How Much Does a Provisional Patent Cost for a Startup?

A provisional patent application for a startup typically costs about $3,000 to $7,500 when a patent professional drafts it, based on published flat fees, or $65 to $325 if you write and file it yourself. The USPTO filing fee is only part of it: $65 for micro entities, $130 for small entities and $325 otherwise. The rest is the time to describe and draw the invention well. What patent firms publish

Not every firm publishes provisional prices, but those that do fall in a fairly narrow band. As of November 2026, published flat fees for a firm-drafted provisional run from about $3,000 to $7,500 in attorney fees, before the USPTO fee. Firms that price by complexity put simple mechanical or software inventions at the low end and complex electrical, biotech or chemical inventions at the high end. One firm says a complex software or medical device provisional can cost about double its $7,500 simple-invention price.

Prices quoted here are each firm's own and change over time. When you compare quotes, check whether drawings, a search and revisions are included.

What drives the cost

  • How complex the invention is. A single mechanism costs less to describe than a system with several interacting parts.
  • How many drawings it needs. Each variation and edge case you want to keep as a fallback needs to be shown.
  • How well-documented the work already is. Specs, diagrams and a running lab notebook mean less time spent reconstructing what you built.
  • The type of technology. Firms that price by complexity charge more for electrical, computer, biotech and chemical inventions than for simple mechanical or software ones.

How to spend less without filing less

  • Keep a lab notebook while you build. A markdown file of what you tried and what worked is the raw material for the draft. See POC and provisional.
  • Sketch before you write. Drawings show possession faster than prose and give you fallback positions. See Draw first, write second.
  • File on the one thing that matters. One provisional on the enabling piece usually beats several thin ones. See Provisional vs. NDA.

What comes after 12 months

A provisional holds your filing date for 12 months. To keep that date, you file a full (non-provisional) application, an international (PCT) application, or both, before the year is up. Budget for this from the start.

Extra claims add fees: each independent claim over three and each claim over 20 costs more. Keeping claims focused keeps fees down.

What should a pre-seed startup budget for patents?

Plan for one provisional on the enabling piece in the first year, about $3,000 to $7,500 at published firm rates, and budget the 12-month decision from the day you file. A full US application adds roughly $5,000 to $15,000 in drafting plus USPTO fees, so know before month 12 whether the signal from your launch justifies it.

The goal is not a portfolio. It is protecting the one thing that changes your bargaining position, cheaply, before you start talking about it.

Common questions

Is the USPTO fee all I pay for a provisional?

Only if you write and file it yourself. The USPTO fee is $65, $130 or $325 depending on entity status. Most of the cost of a provisional is the time to describe and draw the invention well.

Can I file a provisional myself?

Yes. The USPTO accepts provisionals from inventors directly. The risk is a thin description: you can only claim later what the provisional clearly showed, so a rushed filing can protect less than you think.

Do I qualify for the small or micro entity fee?

Most early-stage startups qualify as a small entity, which generally means fewer than 500 employees including affiliates and no obligation to assign or license the invention to a larger company. Micro entity status adds income and prior-filing limits. Check the USPTO rules before claiming either.

What happens if I do not file the full application within 12 months?

The provisional expires and you lose its filing date. A provisional is never examined and never becomes a patent on its own. It only holds your date for 12 months.

Is a cheap provisional worth it?

A provisional is only as useful as what it discloses. Spending less is fine when the description and drawings still show clearly what you built. Spending less by leaving out detail usually costs more later.
